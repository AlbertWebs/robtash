# Reform Initiatives — Homepage Prototype Concept Note

## The challenge

Reform Initiatives’ current digital presence underplays a sophisticated organisation: multi-thematic justice work, transcontinental partnerships, and concrete policy influence (including support to a UN General Assembly resolution). The existing site fragments themes into generic blocks, buries impact, and does not lead with content or a clear invitation to engage.

## Concept thesis

**The homepage should feel like a living platform for African-led repair — not an institutional brochure.**

Visitors should immediately understand *who RI is*, *what field it works in*, *why it matters*, and *how to go deeper* — with content and proof doing the heavy lifting.

## Key decisions

### 1. Brand-first, vision-led hero
The first viewport centres the RI wordmark and the organisational vision, supported by a single mission sentence and two actions: *Explore our work* and *Get involved*. Atmosphere uses deep green and gold grain rather than stock collage or card clutter — signalling seriousness, continuity, and care.

### 2. Cross-thematic spine (not four siloed cards)
Reparatory Justice, Human Rights, Transitional Justice, and Democracy & Governance are presented as one connected system. A selectable theme rail lets users explore each area without pretending the issues are separate. This reflects the brief’s content-first, cross-thematic requirement.

### 3. The 6Rs as distinctive intellectual property
The Reparatory Justice 6Rs framework (Reconnect, Re-Education, Repair, Rebuild, Resist, Reimagine) is available as an expandable layer — present for those who want depth, without overwhelming first-time visitors.

### 4. Impact as editorial narrative
Selected contributions (UNGA resolution, Wakati Wetu, Accra Reparations Conference, Ovaherero & Nama partnership) appear as numbered story rows, not vanity metrics. Credibility comes from *what happened*, not dashboards.

### 5. Content-first insights stream
Calls and articles sit as a reading list with theme tags — modelling how a future CMS-driven homepage would keep the site alive between major campaigns.

### 6. Quiet geography, strong close
Registration countries and partner geographies appear as a calm strip (with a subtle motion marquee), then the page closes on newsletter and contact — one clear engagement path.

## Information architecture (prototype)

- **Home** — full experience (above)
- **Who we are** — values and positioning stub
- **Our work** — thematic depth stub
- **Insights** — content hub stub
- **Get involved** — partnership / contact stub

Secondary pages are intentionally light: enough to demonstrate IA and clickability without pretending the full site is built.

## Visual identity

- **Colours:** Green `#1A510F`, Gold `#D9B97C`, White, Black (corrected from swapped white/black values in the brand table)
- **Type:** Fraunces (display) + Source Sans 3 (body) — expressive but readable
- **Motion:** Hero rise, scroll reveals, theme panel transitions, geography marquee — restrained and purposeful; reduced-motion respected

## What a full build would extend

CMS-backed insights and events; richer media from RI newsletters and field work; partner/funder logos; multilingual pathways; application and donation flows; and deeper programme pages for each theme and the 6Rs.

## How to review

Run the prototype locally (`npm install` then `npm run dev`) and click through the homepage and stub routes. Treat this as a directional working prototype for discussion — structure, tone, and interaction — ahead of full design system and production build.
